Mohammad Reza Daneshvar Garmroodi

I am a Research Scientist at McMaster University, working with Dr. Robin Zhao in the Zhao Lab. I did my PhD at Concordia University under the supervision of Dr. Ida Karimfazli, where I studied mixing in yield-stress fluids.

My research combines curiosity-driven science with nature-inspired engineering, and it draws on complex fluid mechanics, subsurface science, and soft materials. I am a fluid mechanician by training, with a background in computational fluid dynamics and applied mathematics. I build simplified model problems that keep the essential physics of real-world and industrial systems, especially flows with non-Newtonian, multiphase, turbulent, or heat transfer effects. I also work with data-driven methods and reinforcement learning.

At McMaster I work on two topics. The first is subsurface flow and scientific machine learning. I built a GPU-accelerated solver in Julia for flow and contaminant transport in heterogeneous porous media, and a data-driven control framework that combines a Fourier neural operator surrogate, inference of the hidden permeability field from sparse sensor data, and model predictive control. A reinforcement-learning controller is in development, and I compare the simulations with bench-scale experiments in a transparent flow cell. The second is nuclear fuel and repository modelling: a coupled particle–matrix solver in PorePy for radionuclide release from TRISO nuclear fuel, and a coupled thermal-hydraulic-mechanical-chemical (THMC) repository model for SMR fuels in MOOSE.

Before that, I was an Industrial Research Engineer at Concordia, working on industrial R&D in thermal-fluids and CFD. This included a Mitacs-funded project with SINTEF and Jagtech (Norway) on a dual-shear-gun fluid-conditioning system, and a melting solver for heat-spreader designs in electronics thermal management.
