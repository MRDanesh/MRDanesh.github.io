About

I have more than six years of experience in numerical modelling, computational fluid dynamics, thermofluids, machine learning, data-driven methods, and optimization. I have applied my knowledge to multiphase flows, flows in porous media, data-driven control, phase-change materials, and turbulence modelling. I design model problems and algorithms that go beyond default solvers and settings, such as model problems for industrial mixing systems, electronic cooling devices with phase-change materials and highly conductive inserts, and aquifers with unknown, heterogeneous permeability fields. I am motivated, sincere, and hardworking. I learn new methods quickly and see projects through to their goals.

Jun 2026 – present

Research Scientist, McMaster University

Controlling contaminant removal in porous media with an unknown permeability field. In a five-spot Hele-Shaw cell with an unknown permeability field, clean fluid is injected at the centre and pushes the contaminant towards four corner outlets that can each be opened or closed. The permeability field is inferred from sparse sensor data, while the injection rate and the open or closed state of each outlet are controlled to remove the initial contamination with as little clean fluid as possible.

  • Developed an OpenFOAM solver for multiphase Darcy flow in a medium with a heterogeneous permeability field.
  • Fabricated a five-spot Hele-Shaw cell with a heterogeneous permeability field by laser engraving, and ran calibration tests for engraving accuracy.
  • Developed a numerical model problem and generated a dataset on HPC to train a Fourier neural operator as a fast solver.
  • Developed a physics-informed geology estimator that reconstructs the unknown permeability field from sparse sensor data.

Radionuclide release from TRISO nuclear fuel. TRISO fuel particles are designed to hold fission products inside their coating layers. This ongoing project builds an open, reduced-order model of how radionuclides are released from the particles and move through the surrounding fuel matrix. The particle and matrix models are coupled, so each one affects the other.

  • Developing a Canadian coupled particle-matrix nuclear solver in PorePy that covers the Bateman and depletion equations, diffusion, retention and decay through the coating layers, a pressure-driven SiC failure model, and explicit cracks and gaps in a 3D graphite and ceramic matrix, and reporting progress to NSERC every month.
  • Qualified the fuel model over particle populations with Monte Carlo uncertainty and sensitivity analysis.
  • Simulated a coupled thermal-hydraulic-mechanical-chemical (THMC) repository model for SMR fuels in MOOSE, covering heat transport, gas migration and reactive transport, with verification and validation.

Aug 2025 – Jun 2026

Industrial Research Engineer, Concordia University, SINTEF & Jagtech

Optimizing pressure drop and mixing for conditioning oil-based drilling fluids in a Dual Shear Gun system. Oil-based drilling fluids are viscoplastic and must be conditioned during offshore drilling so that their rheology and dispersion stay stable. The Dual Shear Gun is a proposed compact, high-throughput device for fast conditioning: the fluid is sheared hard in a nozzle and then mixed by jets downstream. The goal was to optimize the pressure drop along the nozzle and the placement of the mixing jets to minimize energy consumption.

  • Developed model problems for the nozzle and the mixing chamber.
  • Developed a RANS turbulence model for viscoplastic fluids and validated it against DNS data.
  • Related turbulent stresses to a local maximum stable droplet size using the Kolmogorov–Hinze framework.
  • Proposed an iterative, flow-weighted model that tracks how the droplet-size distribution evolves over successive circulations through the nozzle.
  • Reduced the droplet size by more than a factor of ten and made it more uniform, which makes the fluid more stable.
  • Proposed an optimized nozzle distance that increases mixing by 50% compared with the initial design.
  • Experiments agreed with the numerical predictions.

Buoyancy-driven melting in phase-change materials with embedded heat spreaders. Phase-change materials (PCMs) are used for thermal management in photovoltaic panels and electronic cooling devices, but much of their latent heat goes unused because natural convection in the melted PCM is weak. I built a simplified benchmark that is heated from the top and insulated at the bottom, so heat cannot leave the system and has to be redistributed inside the material. The project studied how high-conductivity inserts improve heat transport.

  • Developed an enthalpy-based conjugate heat-transfer and melting solver in OpenFOAM (code on GitHub).
  • Tested several conductor layouts and measured how they change latent heat use and the onset of buoyancy-driven flow in the melted PCM.
  • Found conductor layouts that lower the hot-side temperature and make melting more uniform.
  • Increased heat transfer efficiency by 200% with the best conductor layout.
